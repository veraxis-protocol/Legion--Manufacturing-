## D4 — Root cause (ranked candidates; not a single verdict)

**Confidence:** the evidence localises the cause to the T7 boring tool, but it cannot separate the two mechanisms below. **Tie for first place.**

1. **Insert T7-0031's edge changes progressively with parts cut** (edge build-up or chipping), and the bore grows.
   - For: 10 consecutive rising points on one insert; slope 0.000535 mm/h; R² 0.876; a 0.027 mm reset at the insert change; NCR-0219's cause.
   - Against: ordinary flank wear on a boring edge tends to cut undersize; no insert wear measurement exists.
2. **The boring head T7 setting creeps during the run**, and resets when the insert is changed and the head re-set.
   - For: the same trend and reset as candidate 1; the oversize direction fits a setting that opens up.
   - Weak point: no head-setting record exists at all.
3. **Coolant 6% → 8% (2026-11-08 16:00) contributes:** untested. There are 0 inspection rows before the change. L-0413 at 8% measured 22.007, which argues against coolant as the sole cause.
4. **Shift 2 practice:** weak. Shift and operator are fully confounded with time on the insert. Shift residuals are ±0.0012 mm (n = 5 each), and there are only 3 failures.

**Why it escaped (system-level root cause, from the evidence):**
- No in-process control on Bore A reacted to the drift. The trend rule was already firing within L-0409 to L-0411, and L-0411 seq 70 (22.019) was beyond the computed UCL of 22.0184 while still in spec.
- CAPA-071's interval is a part count, but parts cut per insert are not recorded anywhere in the inputs. Compliance cannot be shown: the insert had cut at least 211 parts at the first bad part and at least 510 by the end of L-0412, and both are floors.
- CAPA-071 was closed without verifying its effectiveness.

**Verification plan for D4** (the cheapest discriminating test):
- Read and record the T7 head setting on the presetter before the run on T7-0032 continues.
- Measure Bore A every ~50 parts for about the first 300 parts.
- Inspect removed insert T7-0031 for built-up edge or chipping.

A head setting that has not moved, with a bore that still rises, means candidate 1. A setting that moved means candidate 2. The same data, compared with any retained MB-1880 data from before 2026-11-08, tests candidate 3.

## D7 — Prevent recurrence (system level; addresses why CAPA-071 did not hold)

**Why CAPA-071 did not hold, from the evidence:**
- (a) Its 600-part interval was a fixed number set without a measured wear curve.
- (b) No record of parts cut per insert exists, so compliance could not be enforced or shown. This investigation could only establish floors.
- (c) It had no in-process detection: the drift was visible in data the plant already collected but did not chart.
- (d) It was closed with effectiveness "not verified" in April, and the same defect recurred in November.

Re-issuing a 600-part (or any fixed) interval would repeat (a) to (d), so it is not proposed.
