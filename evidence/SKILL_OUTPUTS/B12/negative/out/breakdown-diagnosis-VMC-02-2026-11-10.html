3. Ranked candidate causes

Candidate 1 — Spindle bearing damage or contamination (incl. wear / preload loss)

No work-order citation — reasoning + OEM table

Evidence

  • Grinding noise from the spindle for ~20 min before the alarm (operator). OEM Section 7.4 maps "Grinding noise" to "Bearing damage or contamination".
  • Overtemp alarm: OEM row "Overtemp with rising vibration" points to bearing wear / preload loss, but vibration is unknown (no sensor data), so this row is only partly matched.
  • Bluish discolouration on nose/taper is consistent with heat at the front of the spindle (description only; not diagnostic on its own).
  • Against / unknown: chips looked normal; no vibration data; no history.

First check

Under lockout of the spindle drive: manual rotation feel of the spindle (OEM Section 7.4 check for grinding noise). Rough, notchy or tight rotation supports this candidate. Then, still by hand, run-out at the taper with an indicator against the OEM limit < 3 µm. The vibration-spectrum check is not to be run while the spindle shows grinding noise.

Expected fix and repair path

Spindle bearing replacement or spindle cartridge rebuild/exchange per the OEM; find and remove any contamination path. Before restart, confirm chiller function (Candidate 2), since cooling loss can be the cause of bearing damage rather than a separate fault. Repair scope and part numbers: not in the inputs.

Candidate 2 — Spindle chiller low flow / low fluid level

No work-order citation — reasoning + OEM table

Evidence

  • Overtemp alarm. OEM row "Overtemp, normal vibration" maps to "Chiller low flow / fluid level"; vibration is unknown, so it cannot be ruled in or out from data.
  • Against: the grinding noise is not explained by a chiller fault alone; it can be a consequence (overheated bearings) rather than the cause.

First check

Chiller fluid level and flow (OEM Section 7.4). This is the cheapest test of all: minutes, no spindle disassembly, and it does not require rotating the spindle. Low level or low flow makes this candidate primary (or the root of Candidate 1); normal level and flow largely removes it.

Expected fix and repair path

Restore fluid level, clear the flow restriction or repair the chiller; then still do the Candidate 1 rotation and run-out checks, because ~20 min of grinding may already have damaged the bearings.

Candidate 3 — Drawbar clamp force low (tool slipping in the taper)

No work-order citation — reasoning + OEM table; weakest match

Evidence

  • Overload alarm. OEM row "Overload with tool pull-out marks" maps to "Drawbar clamp force low".
  • Bluish discolouration is reported in the taper area, where a slipping holder would heat (reasoning, not observed fact).
  • Against / unknown: tool pull-out marks are not mentioned in the event note either way; whether they are present is unknown.

First check

Under lockout, remove the tool holder and look at the holder shank and spindle taper for pull-out / fretting marks (visual, minutes). If present, measure with the clamp force gauge against the OEM spec 8.0–9.5 kN. Note: the drawbar is spring-loaded (stored energy); follow the OEM procedure for any drawbar work.

Expected fix and repair path

Restore clamp force per OEM (drawbar spring pack / clamp mechanism service), clean or regrind a damaged taper, replace the damaged holder; check run-out afterwards.

4. The cheapest test that tells them apart

All with the spindle drive locked out; none runs the spindle. Total: a short sequence, cheapest first.

  1. Chiller level and flow (no spindle contact). Low → Candidate 2 primary (bearings still to be checked). Normal → Candidate 2 largely out.
  2. Pull the tool holder and inspect shank + taper for pull-out marks. Marks present → Candidate 3 in play; gauge clamp force (8.0–9.5 kN). No marks → Candidate 3 weak.
  3. Turn the spindle by hand. Rough / notchy / tight → Candidate 1. Then dial run-out at the taper against < 3 µm.

If step 3 is rough, the spindle is not to be run for a vibration spectrum (OEM). If all three are clean, none of the three candidates is supported and the evidence (sensor window, maintenance history) is needed before going further.
