Cheapest test to tell the candidates apart, in this order, about 10 to 15 minutes in total and no parts needed:

  1. Check the chiller fluid level and flow (sight glass or flow indicator). This does not involve the spindle. If the chiller is low again, as in MW-160, that confirms candidate 2 as a contributing cause. It does not rule out candidate 1, because a low chiller does not explain the vibration rise or the grinding.
  2. With the spindle drive locked out, pull the tool holder and inspect its taper and the spindle taper for pull-out marks, fretting or scoring. If you see pull-out marks, candidate 3 (drawbar) moves up. If the holder is clean, candidate 3 moves down.
  3. With the spindle still locked out, turn it by hand (OEM check for grinding). If it feels rough, notchy or tight, candidate 1 (bearing) is confirmed. If it turns smoothly, candidate 1 is weaker and the run-out check comes next.

The next step costs more: a dial indicator run-out check at the taper (the OEM limit is under 3 µm), done with the spindle still not running. The OEM's vibration-spectrum check needs the spindle running, so do not use it here.
