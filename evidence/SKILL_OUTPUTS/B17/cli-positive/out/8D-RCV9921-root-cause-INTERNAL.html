Candidate 1 (leading): supplier lot AS-7781 supplied above hardness specification, and the C of C did not reflect it

AS-7781 produced above 40 HRF at the supplier → supplier lot release / C of C stated 32 HRF and the lot shipped → lot accepted onto PO-30655, 400 pcs issued to WO-8150

Link 1: AS-7781 is above 40 HRF as made.

  • For: 5 of 5 samples above 40 HRF (46–50, mean 48) [F line 3; FB]. The within-lot spread sits wholly above spec: computed LCL 40.69 HRF > 40 HRF max [FB]. This is a lot-level shift, not a few outliers. The parts were found at receiving, before any processing at this plant, so no shift, operator, machine or tool change here could have produced them [F]. The failure is confined to AS-7781: AS-7702 and AS-7744 from the same supplier passed incoming hardness [F line 5].
  • Against: tester ID, calibration date and test location aren't recorded [F], so a measurement bias isn't ruled out. AS-7781 is also the most recent lot tested, so "only this lot fails" can't yet be told apart from "the tester changed since October". 1 failing lot of 3 is a small number. Oil content wasn't tested, so the density/porosity mechanism behind the hardness is unconfirmed [F].

Link 2: the supplier's release did not catch it.

  • For: the C of C states 32 HRF, 16 HRF below our measured mean [F; FB].
  • Against: we hold only the stated value, not the supplier's test records. If our tester reads high, the C of C could be correct.

Link 3: the lot reached the line.

  • For: 400 pcs issued to assembly line 2, WO-8150 [F line 4].
  • Against: nothing on record. This is the weak point of this chain: nothing under For says whether the issue happened before or after the inspection result. The sequence isn't recorded.
  • Confirm: an independent lab measures AS-7781 pieces above 40 HRF, or the supplier's own raw test records for AS-7781 show values above 40.
  • Kill: our tester passes on a certified HRF reference block, a retained AS-7744 piece now reads above 40 on it, or the independent lab reads AS-7781 within 25–40.

Candidate 2: receiving hardness measurement reads high (tester calibration, scale/indenter/load, or test location)

tester or method biased high → readings inflated by 6–10+ HRF → conforming lot rejected

Link 1: tester or method biased high.

  • For: the only positive evidence is the conflict with the C of C (32 HRF stated vs 46–50 measured) [F]. Wall and flange are both about 2.0 mm thick (OD 16, ID 12, flange 2.0) [D]. Thin, curved, porous oil-impregnated sections are sensitive to test location and anvil support. Calibration status isn't recorded [F].
  • Against: the same incoming check passed AS-7702 and AS-7744 [F]. A tester fault would have to begin between the October and November receipts. The bias needed to reconcile with the C of C is large: mean 48 vs 32 stated is 16 HRF [FB].
  • Weak point of this chain: no measurement record shows the tester is off. Link 1 rests on the C of C conflict and on missing records.

Link 2: readings inflated by 6–10+ HRF. No independent evidence either way.

  • Confirm: the tester reads outside tolerance on a certified HRF reference block, or retained AS-7744 pieces now read above 40 on the same tester.
  • Kill: the tester is within tolerance on the reference block, and a re-test of AS-7781 at a documented location still reads above 40.

Candidate 3 (low): identity error, meaning the pieces tested were not BSH-1210 lot AS-7781

  • For: none.
  • Against: receipt RCV-9921, PO-30655 and lot AS-7781 are recorded together in the finding [F].
  • Confirm: the tested pieces' tags or markings don't match RCV-9921 / AS-7781.
  • Kill: the tags match and the pieces match the drawing dimensions [D].

Ranking: Candidate 1 leads, but not decisively. Candidate 2 hasn't been killed; it lacks positive evidence. This is not a tie (Candidate 2 has nothing under For at link 1 beyond the C of C conflict), but confidence is medium.

Cheapest discriminating test: verify the receiving hardness tester on a certified HRF reference block, then re-test 3 retained AS-7781 pieces and 1–2 retained AS-7744 pieces (if any are still in stock) on that tester, at a documented location with proper support. That takes about an hour on equipment already in the building, and it separates Candidate 1 from Candidate 2. Before the claim goes to the supplier, follow with an independent lab hardness and oil-content test on 3 AS-7781 pieces.
