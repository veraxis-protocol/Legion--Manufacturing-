VMC-02: breakdown diagnosis, 10 Nov 2026

Safety: VMC-02, flagged

Follow your plant's lockout procedure before anyone works on this machine. This is not a safety assessment.

  • Do not run the spindle, and do not jog it to 'listen' or to take a vibration spectrum. The OEM manual (Sect. 7.4) says: "Do not run a spindle showing grinding noise." The operator reported grinding for about 20 min before the alarm.
  • Lock out the spindle drive before any hand rotation or inspection (OEM Sect. 7.4). The spindle has not been hand-turned yet, so nobody knows whether it is seized or partly seized.
  • The drawbar clamp is a stored-energy assembly (Belleville spring stack). Use the plant's drawbar procedure for the clamp-force check in candidate 2.
  • The bluish discolouration on the spindle nose and taper means that area overheated. Let it cool before handling it.

Ranked candidate causes

Do these checks in this order. Together they take under 30 minutes and separate all three candidates: (a) chiller level and flow, with no spindle contact; (b) lock out, then turn the spindle by hand; (c) if it turns, run-out at the taper; (d) pull the holder, inspect both tapers, then measure clamp force. A rough spindle at (b) confirms candidate 1. Even then, still do (a) and (d), because they find out why it failed before a new spindle goes in.

1. Spindle bearing damage or loss of preload, a progressive failure confidence: medium advisory

Evidence

  • Sensor data: spindle vibration rose at every sample, 1.8 -> 11.2 (6.2x). Temperature rose with it, 31.0 -> 52.1 (+21.1) °C, and spindle load went 42 -> 71. This is a steady, accelerating climb, which is the pattern progressive wear produces, not a step change.
  • OEM manual Sect. 7.4: "Overtemp with rising vibration → spindle bearing wear / preload loss". "Grinding noise → bearing damage or contamination".
  • Operator: grinding noise for ~20 min before the alarm, and chips looked normal, which points away from a cutting problem. The vibration jump between 15:00 and 15:20 matches when the noise started.
  • Photo description (no image file was supplied): bluish discolouration on the spindle nose and taper. That shows heat at the front of the spindle. It does not say which part produced the heat, and a front-bearing failure and a slipping tool holder would both leave this mark.
  • History: two VMC-02 notes recorded early bearing symptoms, and neither was followed up with a work order in this export (cited below).

Cited work orders (VMC-02 export; each note appears on one asset only, see the template check):

  • MW-118 (2026-03-02): "quarterly PM done. greased ways. spindle sounded a bit rough at 8k, monitor"
  • MW-140 (2026-07-08): "PM. vib reading 2.1 mm/s at 8k (was 1.4 last yr). told sup"

First check

  1. Spindle drive locked out: turn the spindle by hand (about 5 min, OEM Sect. 7.4). If it feels rough, notchy or draggy, the bearings are confirmed. If it turns smoothly, candidates 2 and 3 move up.
  2. If it turns, check run-out at the taper with a test bar and an indicator. The OEM limit is < 3 µm (Sect. 7.4). Out of limit means bearings or a damaged taper.
  3. The vibration spectrum at bearing frequencies, the manual's other check, needs the spindle running. Grinding noise rules that out. Leave it to the spindle rebuilder.

Expected fix and repair path

Remove the spindle cartridge and have it rebuilt or replaced: new bearings, preload reset. Inspect the taper for heat damage and regrind or replace it. Run in, then record a new vibration baseline at a fixed speed. Do this together with candidate 3's chiller check. A new bearing on a spindle with poor cooling can fail the same way. This is a long-lead repair, so get a rebuild quote now.

2. Chiller low on fluid or flow: inadequate spindle cooling that contributed to or caused the failure confidence: low advisory

Evidence

  • History: in September the chiller was found low on fluid and topped up. The note does not say why it was low, so a leak was never ruled out (cited below).
  • OEM manual Sect. 7.4: "Overtemp, normal vibration → chiller low flow / fluid level".
  • Against it as the sole cause: vibration did not stay normal (1.8 -> 11.2 (6.2x)), so by the manual's own table this is not a pure cooling fault.
  • The sensor data cannot test this. coolant_pressure_bar held at 19.8 - 20.1 bar, but that is very likely cutting-coolant pressure, not chiller flow. There is no chiller channel in the export.

Cited work orders (VMC-02 export; each note appears on one asset only, see the template check):

  • MW-160 (2026-09-23): "spindle hot after long cycle. checked chiller - low on fluid topped up. temp ok after"

First check

Check the chiller fluid level and flow, and look for leaks (about 5 min; the spindle does not need to be touched, but follow your lockout procedure). If the level is low again after the September top-up, there is a leak, and cooling becomes the likely root cause behind candidate 1, not an alternative to it.

Expected fix and repair path

Find and fix the fluid loss. Verify chiller flow against the OEM spec, which is not in the excerpt supplied. Add chiller level to the PM (see the PM draft below).

3. Drawbar clamp force low: tool holder slipping or fretting in the taper confidence: low advisory

Evidence

  • History: in May a spindle alarm was traced to low drawbar clamp force, and the Belleville stack was adjusted, not replaced (cited below).
  • An overload alarm and heat colour at the taper are both consistent with a holder slipping.
  • OEM manual Sect. 7.4: "Overload with tool pull-out marks → drawbar clamp force low".
  • Against it: no tool pull-out marks are reported, chips looked normal, and grinding noise plus a steadily rising vibration fit bearings better.

Cited work orders (VMC-02 export; each note appears on one asset only, see the template check):

  • MW-131 (2026-05-19): "spindle alarm 2nd shift. found drawbar clamp low, adjusted belleville stack. ran ok"

First check

Pull the tool holder from WO-8120 op 20 and look at its taper and the spindle taper for fretting or pull-out marks (about 5 min). Then measure clamp force with the gauge. The OEM spec is 8.0-9.5 kN (Sect. 7.4). Belleville stack: stored energy, use the drawbar procedure. Clamp force in spec and no marks rules this candidate out.

Expected fix and repair path

Replace the Belleville stack rather than adjust it again, because May's adjustment did not hold if the force is low now. Clean the taper, or regrind it if fretted. Replace a damaged tool holder.
