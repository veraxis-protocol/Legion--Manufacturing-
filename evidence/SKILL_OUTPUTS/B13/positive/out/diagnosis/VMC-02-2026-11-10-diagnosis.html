Ranked candidate causes advisory, technician step pending

1. Spindle bearing damage / preload loss (progressive wear) confidence: high

Evidence. Sensor: vibration rose every sample from 1.8 / 11.2 mm/s (6.22x), temperature rose 21.1 °C, load 42 / 71 %; the rise accelerated in the last 24 minutes (rates: 13:00-13:30: 0.003; 13:30-14:00: 0.017; 14:00-14:30: 0.023; 14:30-15:00: 0.05; 15:00-15:20: 0.165; 15:20-15:24: 0.825). That is the steady-climb shape of progressive wear, and the OEM tree's first row, Overtemp with rising vibration → spindle bearing wear / preload loss. Operator: grinding noise about 20 minutes before the alarm (manual: Grinding noise → bearing damage or contamination). Photo description (no photo file): bluish discolouration at the spindle nose / taper, consistent with heat there but not, on its own, a diagnosis. History: MW-118 (2026-03-02, PM) "spindle sounded a bit rough at 8k, monitor"; MW-140 (2026-07-08, PM) "vib reading 2.1 mm/s at 8k (was 1.4 last yr). told sup". Two PMs logged the same developing condition and no corrective work order follows in the export.

First check. Under lockout, rotate the spindle by hand and feel for roughness or notchiness (manual 7.4, Grinding noise row), then indicate run-out at the taper against the OEM limit of < 3 µm (manual 7.4, row 1). Do not run the spindle for a vibration spectrum while grinding is present. Rough rotation or run-out over 3 µm confirms this candidate and makes candidates 2 and 3 contributors at most.

Expected fix and repair path. Spindle bearing replacement or spindle cartridge rebuild per OEM; inspect the taper and spindle nose for heat damage given the discolouration; after rebuild, record a baseline vibration reading. Check candidate 2 (cooling) before restart so the new bearings are not run hot.

Cited: MW-118, MW-140.

2. Spindle chiller low flow / low fluid level confidence: medium as a contributor, low as the sole cause

Evidence. MW-160 (2026-09-23, CM) "spindle hot after long cycle. checked chiller - low on fluid topped up. temp ok after": the same machine overheated about seven weeks earlier from low chiller fluid; no follow-up on why the level dropped. Against it as the sole cause: the OEM tree's chiller row is Overtemp, normal vibration, and vibration here rose 6.22x. The historian export has no chiller flow or level channel; coolant pressure (19.8 to 20.1 bar) is flat but that is cutting-coolant, not the spindle chiller, so it does not test this candidate.

First check. Chiller fluid level and flow (manual 7.4, row 2). Five minutes, no spindle rotation needed. Low level again points to a leak or consumption since MW-160 and to a cooling contribution to the overtemp.

Expected fix and repair path. Restore level and flow, find where the fluid is going (leak check on lines and fittings), and confirm before any spindle restart.

Cited: MW-160.

3. Drawbar clamp force low confidence: low

Evidence. MW-131 (2026-05-19, CM) "spindle alarm 2nd shift. found drawbar clamp low, adjusted belleville stack. ran ok": a prior spindle alarm on this machine traced to the drawbar. The OEM row is Overload with tool pull-out marks. Against it: no pull-out marks were reported, the operator said chips looked normal, and the load rise tracked the vibration and temperature rise rather than appearing as a step. The taper discolouration cannot tell heat from bearings apart from fretting at a poorly clamped holder from a description alone.

First check. With the drive locked out, inspect the tool holder taper for pull-out or fretting marks, then measure clamp force with the gauge against OEM spec 8.0–9.5 kN (manual 7.4, row 3).

Expected fix and repair path. Re-adjust or replace the Belleville stack per OEM procedure (stored spring energy); clean and inspect the taper.

Cited: MW-131.
